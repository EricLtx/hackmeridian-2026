# Security baseline of the Open Window contract (crate `night-window`)

Date: 6 October 2026, revised on 13, 15, 18 and 20 October 2026 · Status: **decisions S1 to S34 and DORA D1, D2 taken; version 4 runs on testnet as rc4, frozen at the tag `audit-v4-rc4`; the rc5 candidate (the fixes of the security pass of 20 October 2026: decisions S30 to S34 and the amendments to S1, S2, S3, S7, S8, S9, S17, S20, S22, S25, S26, S28 and S29) is implemented and tested, under adversarial review before it becomes the tag `audit-v4-rc5`; external audit to come** · Business reference: the internal framing study, kept outside the public repository · STRIDE threat model: [threat-model-stride.md](threat-model-stride.md)

This document sets out what is already in place in the code and the toolchain, what the specification will have to settle, and the review rules that apply to every change of the contract. It follows the review grid of the official `stellar-dev` skill (file `security.md`), applied to the case of a repo.

---

## 1. What must be protected

| Asset | Where it lives | Who can lose it |
|---|---|---|
| Stablecoins deposited in offers | Contract balance, broken down by `Offer(id)` entry | Lenders |
| Fund shares locked in repos | Contract balance, broken down by `Repo(id)` entry | Borrowers |
| Registry of accepted funds (oracle, haircut) | `Fund(address)` entries | Everyone: a bad oracle or a zero haircut allows borrowing more than the value of the shares |
| Administration key | `Admin` entry (instance storage) | Everyone: the admin changes the registry and the code |
| Contract code | WASM referenced by the instance | Everyone: a malicious upgrade empties the contract |

The contract is **value-bearing** as soon as an offer is deposited. The requirements of section 6 (pause, admin governance) apply before any real deposit, not only before mainnet.

## 2. Threat model

### 2.1 What the attacker controls

Standard platform assumptions, taken as they are:

- every argument passed to the contract's functions;
- the order and timing of transactions (it sees an offer arrive and can take it or front-run it);
- every account except those whose signature it does not hold;
- the deployment of contracts that mimic an interface (a fake SEP-40 oracle, a fake SEP-41 token);
- the shape of the on-chain state it can legally create: number of entries, and even the TTL of **our** entries, which it can extend.

### 2.2 Actors and trust granted

| Actor | Trust | Consequence for the contract |
|---|---|---|
| Borrower | None | Every function it calls checks its `require_auth` and its bounds |
| Lender | None | Same; an offer can only be withdrawn by its author and only for the part not lent |
| Administrator | High, to be framed | It chooses the oracles, the haircuts and the code. Section 6 proposes to frame it (multisig, time delay) |
| Fund issuer | External, imposed | The shares are permissioned tokens: a permissioned Soroban token or a classic asset with authorization, depending on the issuer. The issuer can freeze or claw back a balance, including the contract's |
| Stablecoin issuer | External, imposed | It can freeze the contract's or a borrower's balance of its coin: a repurchase in that coin then fails while the seizure stays open, and withdrawals in it fail too. The cash leg has no equivalent of S9 (threat model DoS.8, open) |
| SEP-40 oracle | Allowlisted by the admin | Its price is external data: freshness and bounds to check at every read |
| Soroban host | Full | Provides auth, blocks reentrancy, meters resources |

### 2.3 What the platform guarantees

- No `delegatecall`: no foreign code runs in the contract's context.
- No reentrancy: the host blocks reentrant calls, direct or indirect. External calls remain failure and budget boundaries; the order of writes still needs care.
- Protection against authorization replay: nonces and expirations are handled by the host.
- Authorization is **explicit and optional**: a forgotten `require_auth` is the flaw to look for first.

## 3. Contract invariants

These invariants are the basis of the property tests and of the review. They are written here in plain language; the specification will formalize them.

| # | Invariant | Status |
|---|---|---|
| I1 | The contract's stablecoin balance is at least equal to the sum of the available amounts of all offers; the outstanding of each currency equals the sum of the open principals | **In place**, checked by `check_invariants` after 300 random operations; since rc5 the monitor also compares each stablecoin's balance with what the offers hold (a deficit is critical and pauses entries, a surplus is a warning) |
| I2 | Every share held for a position belongs to exactly one open repo or to one deferred release; the contract's share balance for a fund is at least the sum of the shares of the open repos and of the pending releases. The tests check equality; a transfer to the contract from outside a repo leaves a surplus that no function moves out, which harms no position | **In place**, tests and reconciliation detector of the monitoring: since rc5 a deficit is critical and pauses entries, a surplus is a warning with its size |
| I3 | At opening, principal ≤ value of the shares × (1 − haircut), with a non-stale price | **In place**, `open_lends_exactly_the_ceiling_and_not_one_unit_more` |
| I4 | Before maturity, only the borrower can close the repo (repurchase). After maturity, repurchase remains possible as long as the seizure has not taken place, and only the lender can seize | **In place**, intruder tests and seizure-before-maturity tests |
| I5 | A repo changes state exactly once: `Open` → `Repurchased` or `Open` → `Defaulted`. No going back | **In place**, `RepoNotOpen` on any second transition |
| I6 | No function iterates over the set of offers or repos; every call touches a bounded number of entries | **In place by construction**: individual `Offer(u64)` and `Repo(u64)` keys, no global index |
| I7 | Only the governor changes the registry, the envelope, the term ceiling, the governor and the code; a fund's steward only changes its limits, its lenders, its freeze and the reference downward | **In place**: `require_governor` and the steward role are loaded from storage, never from an argument |
| I8 | Error codes and exported type variants are never renumbered or reshaped; we append | **In place** as a rule (comment in `lib.rs`, AGENTS.md) |
| I9 | A repo's terms (rate, maturity, applied haircut) are frozen at opening; removing a fund from the registry or changing its haircut does not affect open repos | **In place**, `terms_are_frozen_at_opening` |

## 4. Vulnerability classes applied to Open Window

| Class | Risk for this contract | Response |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Missing authorization | Withdrawal of an offer by a third party, seizure by a non-lender, oracle change by a non-admin | **In place**: role loaded from storage for every privileged function; `lender` on `create_offer` and `withdraw_offer`, `borrower` on `open_repo`, borrower and lender loaded from `Repo` in `repurchase` and `seize`; one intruder test per function |
| 2. Authorization replay through an intermediary | `open_repo` will make a `transfer` of shares on behalf of the borrower. Without the borrower's `require_auth` **in `open_repo` itself**, anyone can consume a pre-signed authorization | Rule: every layer that exercises an address's authority re-authenticates it. Mandatory test with targeted `mock_auths` and check of the `env.auths()` tree |
| 3. Re-initialization | Capture of the admin by a second `initialize` | **Ruled out**: `__constructor` constructor, no `initialize` function |
| 4. Arbitrary contract call | Fake oracle, fake share token, exotic stablecoin | Funds and oracles **allowlisted** by the admin (registry). Stablecoins of offers allowlisted too, in their own registry (S5, S29), with 7 decimals only since rc5 (S31) |
| 5. Arithmetic overflow | Interest computation (`principal × rate × term`), price × shares conversion with decimals | Lint `clippy::arithmetic_side_effects` set to `deny`: every operation must go through `checked_*` or `saturating_*` and return a typed error. `overflow-checks = true` in release **and** in test. `i128` amounts: refuse `≤ 0` on input. Since rc5 the value of the shares splits shares and price into whole units and remainders, so 18-decimal shares priced by an 18-decimal feed no longer overflow (S32) |
| 6. Key collision | An offer overwritten by a repo | **Ruled out**: typed `DataKey` enumeration; unique type names in the crate |
| 7. Check-then-act race | The lender withdraws its offer between the simulation and the execution of an `open_repo`; price that moves between two ledgers | Checks and writes in the same invocation; the borrower passes bounds (requested `principal`, term) and the contract refuses rather than adjusting silently |
| 8. TTL and archiving | An archived repo blocks repurchase or seizure; a TTL used as a maturity | The maturity is **stored in the value** (`Repo.maturity`) and compared with `ledger().timestamp()`, never derived from the TTL. TTL of `Fund`, `Offer`, `Repo` entries extended at every write; instance TTL extended on the admin paths |
| 9. Trust in an external value | Stale price, zero or aberrant price, oracle returning `None` | **In place**: freshness against the ledger clock, future timestamp refused, model-based corridor, FX band, backup feed that must agree; typed refusal rather than a default price over the weekend. Since rc5: corridor growth bounded by the NAV age and covered by the haircut (S30), the FX rate taken on the lender's side of its band (S22), feeds of at least 6 decimals with bounds rounded inward (S32) |
| 10. Resource exhaustion | Accepted-fund list of an offer too long; iteration over positions | **In place**: I6; at most eight funds per offer (S10); at most sixteen backup feeds and eight pending caps; 200-repo test at constant cost |
| 11. Custom accounts | Not applicable: the contract does not implement `__check_auth` | — |

### "Token consumer" review, specific to fund shares

| Point | Response |
|---|---|
| Accepted tokens | Allowlisted: shares through the fund registry; stablecoins through their own registry (S5, S29) |
| Decimals | Queried and stored at registration (`decimals()` of the token and of the oracle), never assumed. Since rc5 a stablecoin must have 7 (S31) and a NAV or FX feed at least 6 (S32) |
| Amount received after `transfer` | For permissioned tokens, check the balance before and after the transfer in `open_repo` and `create_offer` rather than trusting the requested amount |
| SAC and permissioned-token specifics | **The risk specific to this project**: an issuer that freezes the contract's balance, removes a beneficiary from its allowlist or claws back the shares blocked the repurchase; a lender not authorized to hold the shares made the seizure fail. **Handled in version 3**: lenders are approved by the steward (S8); if the token refuses the transfer at repurchase or seizure (freeze, allowlist removal, clawback, deauthorization by the asset contract), the debt settles and the shares are delivered as soon as the issuer allows it (S9). Tested with the allowlisted token and with a revocable asset contract. Since rc5 a seizure after the lender's revocation also settles, and the shares wait for an approval (S8) |
| `transfer_from` flow | Not planned: transfers are made with `transfer` with the authorization of the address concerned in the same transaction |
| Standard events | Tokens emit their own; the contract will emit its own lifecycle events (offer created, repo opened, repurchased, seized, withdrawal) |

## 5. Controls already in place in the code

Status as of 14 October 2026, full lifecycle implemented; updated on 20 October 2026 for the rc5 candidate.

- **Initialization.** Atomic constructor with the governor, who is also the first guardian; no initialization window.
- **Roles.** Every privileged function loads its role from storage and calls `require_auth` on it: governor (registry, risk envelope, currency caps, upgrade, unpause), guardian (pause of entries, cancellation of proposals, removal of a backup feed, handing over its role), steward of each fund (approved lenders, settings inside the envelope, fund freeze, lowering of the reference). Two-step governor transfer. Fund admission signed by the governor and the steward in the same call.
- **Authority of the parties.** `require_auth` on the lender in `create_offer` and `withdraw_offer`, on the borrower in `open_repo`; in `repurchase` and `seize`, the address is loaded from the stored repo, never taken as an argument.
- **Prices.** Oracles read only from the registry; freshness enforced and future timestamps refused; model-based corridor (S2) whose growth stops at one NAV age, covered by the fund's haircut (S30, rc5); cross price with its own FX band and FX haircut, the rate taken on the lender's side of the band (S22, rc5); reference that never falls and only rises at the pace of the model; feeds of at least 6 decimals, bounds rounded inward (S32, rc5).
- **Bounds.** Haircuts strictly below 100 % (`Bps` type); risk envelope bounding every steward setting; caps per repo, per fund and per currency; term from one hour to 30 days; at most eight funds per offer; stablecoins of 7 decimals only (S31, rc5).
- **Transfers.** Every token inflow is checked by the balance before and after (`TransferMismatch` otherwise).
- **Pause.** Only `create_offer` and `open_repo` consult it; `repurchase`, `withdraw_offer` and `seize` always remain available.
- **Storage.** Typed keys, one entry per fund, offer and repo; TTL extended at every write (threshold 30 days, extension to 120 days); maturities as timestamps.
- **Errors and events.** 51 typed error codes (1 and 34 are reserved), no `unwrap` or `panic!` (`deny` lints); one event per state change; since rc5 every opening also records its valuation (`RepoValued`, S33).
- **Real-time profile.** A 64 KiB WASM stack, guarded by a CI canary, and a budget per call checked on the release WASM (S34, rc5).
- **Tests.** Host tests for the contract (their number for the rc5 candidate is in [audit-scope.md](audit-scope.md)), among them the upgrade of the rc2 WASM deployed on testnet followed by `migrate` with a repo open: intruder refused on every privileged function, `env.auths()` trees checked, events compared in XDR, every error path, invariants checked after 300 random operations, constant footprint of `open_repo` (16 entries read, 9 written; 18 read with a backup feed), full cycle with a real allowlisted token and a real price feed.

## 6. Decisions taken at framing (13 October 2026)

Each decision below was taken while refining the framing, on the basis of verified facts: code of regulated issuers' contracts already deployed on Stellar, network documentation, public incidents, DeFi security standards published by the SDF on 20 July 2026, ESMA call for evidence of 9 October 2026. The specification formalizes them; every point must have a test.

| # | Subject | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Price freshness | The price read must carry a `timestamp` at most N hours old; N is a registry parameter, per fund, set to span a weekend if the feed does not publish on Saturday. Absent or stale price: refusal (`Error::NoPrice`, new error `StalePrice`), never a silent adjustment. **Version 3**: one backup feed per fund, admitted with two keys after the delay, replaces a stale or failing primary feed; when both are fresh, they must agree within `max_divergence_bps` (at most the corridor floor), otherwise `PriceDivergence`. A fresh but invalid backup makes the computation fail (refusal by default), a stale backup is ignored; its maximum age is capped by that of the fund's NAV. **rc5**: a backup is a second source, never the primary feed itself (`OutsideEnvelope`), and its proposal and its apply each need a fresh primary that it agrees with (otherwise the primary's `StalePrice` or `NoPrice`, or `PriceDivergence`): a stale primary no longer lets a backup in unchecked. The tolerance stays capped by the envelope's corridor floor, not by the fund's own floor (open point of the security pass). Tests `the_primary_feed_cannot_be_its_own_fallback`, `a_fallback_is_installed_only_against_a_fresh_primary` |
| S2 | Price bounds | **Model-based corridor**, not a relative deviation from the previous price. An accumulating fund share follows a predictable trajectory: the NAV read must lie between `reference × (1 − floor)` and `reference × (1 + floor + max_yield × elapsed / year)`, `elapsed` being the time since the reference, counted over at most the fund's NAV age bound since rc5 (S30). The reference does not follow the oracle: it never falls and rises at most along the model (`model_path`), by at most one NAV age of growth per update since rc5, anyone being able to advance it; the steward can only lower it. A faulty feed can therefore push it up no faster than the model's maximum yield, and never down, even by publishing at every ledger (finding H1 of the review of 14 October 2026, dedicated test); since rc5 the fund's haircut covers the whole corridor (S30). The exchange rate of a cross price has its own band around a reference fixed at the two-key admission (S22). The YieldBlox incident of 22 February 2026 ($10.2M) bypassed a relative-deviation check because the two windows compared were already manipulated |
| S3 | Interest and rounding | `principal × rate_bps × term / (10,000 × 365 × 86,400)` in checked `i128`, rounded in the lender's favor. **Amended on 14 October 2026**: the term counts per started hour, one hour at least, and interest keeps running after maturity until repurchase or seizure (decision Q2: lateness is not free). Within one hour, the started hour keeps the amount signed by the borrower stable between the simulation and the execution of its transaction; across an hour boundary the amount grows, and a repurchase signed before the boundary fails after it. A term in whole hours puts maturity, from which the lender may seize, on such a boundary: the terminal warns when the next hour is about to start. Principal and interest flow back into the offer, where the lender withdraws them: no transfer to a lender's address on the borrower's exit path (finding H2) |
| S4 | Borrowing ceiling | `shares × price × (10,000 − haircut) / (10^oracle_decimals × 10,000)`, decimals of the share token and of the stablecoin taken into account, rounded down |
| S5 | Accepted stablecoins | Registry kept by the admin, as for funds. **MiCA-compliant allowlist by construction** for any European use: EURC, EURCV, EURAU, USDC. USDT0 and PYUSD excluded: ESMA's opinion of 8 October 2026 expects national authorities to obtain from MiCA providers the end of services on non-compliant stablecoins within three months, that is around 8 January 2027; it goes through national authorities and is not directly binding (corrected on 15 October 2026) |
| S6 | Emergency pause | `pause` by the guardian or the governor, `unpause` by the governor alone. The pause blocks `create_offer` and `open_repo`, **never** `repurchase`, `withdraw_offer` or `seize`: we stop entries, not exits. A single signer is enough to pause; the power is narrowly delimited |
| S7 | Admin governance | Admin held by a native Stellar multisignature account with thresholds, with at least one signer outside the maintainer (**in place on testnet**, 2 of 2, on 15 October 2026; a single key is refused by the network). **Version 3: time delay built into the contract**, rather than an external TimelockController. Upgrade, replacement of a fund, backup feed, re-anchor of a reference, new guardian, increase of the term ceiling, loosening of the envelope, of a fund's limits or of a currency cap: proposal, delay (48 hours by default, settable before the first admission then only upward), application by anyone within the following seven days, cancellation by the guardian (and the steward for what concerns its fund). Every tightening is immediate and erases the pending loosening; a change of governor erases its proposals, except a stablecoin registration, kept per token, which since rc5 only the governor in office applies (S29). Proposals timed under bootstrap rules go at the first admission, cancelled by the contract: a governor handover and, since rc5, an upgrade, so that every upgrade applied once value is at stake has waited for the delay in force (test `the_first_admission_drops_a_bootstrap_upgrade`). The guardian can hand over its role at any time; the governor can replace it alone only after 30 days. The pause and `remove_fund` remain immediate. Two-step admin transfer, **in place**: `set_admin` proposes, `accept_admin` requires the successor's signature. Roles **in place**: governor, guardian (pause of entries only), one steward per fund (the issuer), who approves lenders and tunes its fund inside the risk envelope; the admission of a fund requires the governor and the steward, and a replacement also requires the steward in place. **Audit candidate rc2**: an optional trusted third party (*trustee*), fixed before the first admission and independent of all roles (checked in both directions), co-signs every upgrade and every forced guardian change and can veto them, as it can the governor transfer; it replaces only itself, with the successor's agreement, and a hand-over erases what it had co-signed; a lost trustee key forbids any upgrade, by design. Lender approvals carry the steward that granted them: after a steward change, new offers and loans wait for a new approval, open positions remain deliverable (S8). The replacement of a steward's backup key waits for the delay; the governor can cancel it, hence block it, never choose it |
| S8 | Seized shares | **Lenders are necessarily holders authorized by the issuer**: the steward approves them fund by fund, the approval is re-checked at every opening, and the seizure goes to the lender or to another approved address. No presentation for redemption by the contract is needed. **Amended for rc5** (security pass of 20 October 2026, threat model DoS.7): a revocation stops the lender's new offers and loans at once but no longer the seizure of its open repos, which before rc5 left the borrower a free option. The lender of record seizes without a current approval: the default settles (outstanding released, repo `Defaulted`, `RepoSeized`), and when the lender is not approved the shares wait in the contract as a `PendingRelease` (`ReleaseDeferred`) until the steward approves it again, after a fresh attestation (S25), or until the lender redirects them to an approved holder (`redirect_release`); `release` returns `false` meanwhile. Any other recipient needs its own approval (`LenderNotApproved`). Seized shares never reach an unapproved holder or the steward (`StewardCannotLend`). Limit: after a revocation followed by `remove_fund`, no approval can be granted until a re-admission, so the shares wait unless a holder approved before the removal exists; a recovery path for a removed fund is left to version 2. Tests `a_revoked_lender_still_seizes_and_its_shares_wait_for_an_approval`, `a_revoked_lender_redirects_its_seized_shares_to_an_approved_holder`, `a_revoked_lender_seizes_on_a_removed_fund`, `seized_shares_never_go_to_the_steward` |
| S9 | Freeze or clawback by the issuer during a repo | The debt remains due. **Version 3**: if the share token refuses the transfer at repurchase or seizure (freeze of the contract, allowlist removal, clawback), the debt settles anyway (repayment into the offer, outstanding released, interest stopped) and the shares owed are recorded in the beneficiary's name (`PendingRelease`). Anyone can retry the delivery (`release`) as soon as the issuer allows it; the beneficiary can redirect it. The shares can only go to the recorded beneficiary, once. Replayed on testnet on 15 October 2026. Since rc5 seized shares also wait this way while their lender is not approved (S8). Limits: the shares of a fund are pooled in the contract, so a clawback aimed at one position is borne by whichever position closes last (threat model Elev.5, open, interface change); and the cash leg has no equivalent: a stablecoin issuer that freezes the contract or a borrower makes the repurchase in that coin fail while the seizure stays open (DoS.8, open, interface change), which the monitor watches since rc5 |
| S10 | Resource bounds | At most 8 funds per offer; a single share transfer and a single stablecoin transfer per operation; no loop over the set of offers or repos (I6) |
| S11 | Minimum term | 1 hour |
| S12 | Identifiers | Monotonic `NextOfferId` and `NextRepoId` counters in `u64`, never reused |
| S13 | Events | One event per state transition, identifiers and addresses as topics, for audit, indexing and reconciliation on the issuer's side |
| S14 | Schema migration | Stored schema version; idempotent `migrate` function reserved to the admin; data migration mechanism of SDK 28 |
| S15 | End of upgradability | The contract remains upgradable behind the time delay and published roles. Commitment: give up upgrades after the pilot if an issuer asks for it. The delay has been built into the contract since version 3. Since rc2, the renunciation is obtained through the *trustee*: named to the issuer or to a third party, no upgrade passes without its signature, and a deliberately destroyed trustee key freezes the code for good. An explicit renunciation function remains possible by upgrading to code without `upgrade`, if an issuer asks for it |
| S16 | Lender's reserve | **Amended 18 October 2026.** The lender-operator holds a reserve off chain, by agreement, sized from the maximum-loss scenarios of 6.1 (about 5 % of the offers it deposits, the benchmark of the SDF DeFi standards), before any real deposit on the main network. The contract holds no buffer: a buffer funded by the fund issuer would be external support under Art. 35 of the MMF Regulation, and a buffer in the contract would be an interface change deferred to a later version |
| S17 | Monitoring plan | Written before any real deposit, derived from the threat model: triggers on the contract's events, on price deviation and on balances; alert within minutes; automatic pause possible on alert. Required by tranche 2 of the community fund. **rc5**: with two RPC endpoints configured, every automatic action is taken only once the second endpoint's own reading confirms it, otherwise a critical alert asks a human; a veto is automatic only with the guardian's or the trustee's key on the monitoring host |
| S18 | Launch caps | Per repo, per fund and per currency (no all-currency cap in the code), derived from the maximum-loss scenarios (6.1). The total cap is aligned with the threshold of the Audit Bank's first free follow-up audit, $10M of locked value |
| S19 | Designated offer | An offer can be marked "sponsor" by the steward of a fund it accepts; the mark is indicative, the interface shows it first, with no on-chain matching (invariant I6). It follows the same rules as any offer. No exit privilege |
| S20 | Rent of the contract code | Operations extend the contract instance, never the code entry: rent grows with size, and extending a 75 KB code to 120 days cost about 530 XLM on testnet on 14 October 2026, beyond the maximum fee of a transaction. The user who crossed the threshold would have paid that rent. The operator now extends the code in slices of 30 days at most (TTL detector of the monitoring); test `operations_extend_the_instance_but_not_the_code`. Limit found by the security pass of 20 October 2026 (threat model DoS.9): on mainnet, restoring an archived code entry could cost more than one transaction can carry, so the code must never be left to archive; a simulated restore is due before any mainnet deployment. **Decided, in place in rc5's tooling**: the monitor alerts when the code entry has less than 90 days to live (14 days for the other entries), and the keeper extends it by 30 days, up to 120 days, under a fee cap of 300 XLM. At the testnet rate read on 20 October 2026, 7.15 XLM a day for the rc4 code, a 30-day slice cost about 215 XLM, so the keeper's float alert is set at 250 XLM |
| S21 | Re-anchor of the price reference | The NAV reference leaves its model trajectory, and the FX reference moves at all, only through two paths signed with two keys: the admission of a fund and the re-anchor (`propose_reanchor`). The re-anchor records the feeds' readings at the proposal, accepted only near the values the two signers named (`expected_nav` within the corridor floor, `expected_fx` within the FX band), bound to those feeds, and installs them after the delay only if the NAV has followed the model since the proposal and the rate is still inside its band. A fund replacement or a re-admission keep the reference; a new feed must read inside the corridor in place. Nobody chooses the moment of a re-anchor at the day's price |
| S22 | FX band and FX haircut | The FX band of a cross-priced fund (`fx.band_bps`, around its FX reference) cannot exceed its FX haircut (`fx.haircut_bps`). Without this rule, a false FX feed at +4.9 % passed a 5 % band while the minimum haircut is 1.5 % (economic model of 17 October 2026, I16). Refusal at admission and at replacement, `FxBandExceedsHaircut` (48). Operational consequence: an EUR/USD drift beyond the band since the reference makes honest rates fail (`PriceOutOfBand`) until a two-key re-anchor (S21). The rule is also checked when limits are set (`set_fund_limits`): a fund admitted before it with a band wider than its haircut must first be replaced (two-key proposal, delay), which the rc4 upgrade of the testnet instance showed on 18 October 2026. **Amended for rc5** (security pass of 20 October 2026, threat model Tamper.6): the band is centred on the reference, which only a two-key re-anchor moves, while the market drifts inside it; read as published, a wrong rate at the low edge with the market near the top overvalued the shares by about two bands. The valuation now divides the NAV by the higher of the rate and the reference (`oracle::value`): a rate below the reference never raises the price, so while the market stays inside the band an admitted rate overvalues the shares by at most the market's move above the reference, at most the band, which the FX haircut covers. The cost falls on the borrower: when the market really sits below the reference, the shares are valued up to one band low until a re-anchor. Residual: an FX feed frozen inside the band while the market moves beyond it is bounded only by the feed's integrity and the monitor (`feed-flat`). Tests `a_wrong_fx_rate_inside_the_band_lends_no_more_than_the_true_value`, `fx_leg_is_priced_at_the_higher_of_the_rate_and_the_reference` |
| S23 | Sole lender mode | An instance can fix, once, before the first admission and the first offer, the only address allowed to create offers (`set_sole_lender`); the choice is irrevocable, checked at every `create_offer` without an extra entry, and held by an account with rotating signers, never a bare key. Errors `NotSoleLender` (41), `SoleLenderFixed` (44) |
| S24 | The steward never lends | A fund's steward, and its last installed steward, cannot be attested, approved, lender of an offer listing that fund, recipient of a seizure or of a redirection; an approved lender cannot become steward or steward backup (MMF Regulation, Article 35: no external support). Error `StewardCannotLend` (40) |
| S25 | Co-signed attestation before approval | An approval requires an attestation signed by the lender and by the steward in place (`attest_lender`), which records only the hash of an off-chain declaration; revocation erases the attestation; the approval carries the address of the steward that granted it, and new offers and loans require the approval of the steward in place, while open positions stay deliverable under the approval of a previous steward and, since rc5, seizable after a revocation, the shares then waiting for an approval (S8). Error `AttestationMissing` (42) |

| S27 | Risk envelope | The governor sets bounds (minimum haircuts, corridor floor, yield, ages, FX band) that no fund setting may cross (`RiskEnvelope`, `set_envelope`); the default values are those of `default_envelope`; a tightening is immediate, a loosening waits for the delay; configurations already admitted are not re-checked. | S28 | Trusted third party (*trustee*) | An optional trustee is named once, before the first admission, by the governor and by itself; it must be independent of all roles, checked in both directions, and since rc5 of the roles about to change hands: it may be neither the proposed governor, the proposed guardian nor the sentinel, `apply_guardian_change` checks again, and naming or handing over the trustee drops a pending forced guardian change. `accept_admin` and `apply_forced_guardian_change` do not check again: since rc5 no proposal can name the trustee or the sentinel, so only a proposal made under rc4 could; its signature is required for every upgrade proposal, every forced guardian change and, since rc4, the schema migration; it can cancel these proposals as well as the governor hand-over; it replaces only itself, with its successor's signature, and a hand-over erases what it had co-signed; a lost trustee key forbids any upgrade, by design (this is the renunciation of S15). | S30 | Corridor growth window and haircut cover | **rc5** (security pass of 20 October 2026, threat model Tamper.7). Before rc5 the corridor's top grew with the time since the last reference update, without bound, and nothing related the haircut to the corridor: after a quiet spell, a faulty or issuer-run feed could price the shares above their value by more than the haircut. The corridor and the model path now grow over at most the fund's NAV age bound (`nav_max_age_secs`) since the reference, so one update moves the reference by at most one window of growth. A reference nobody moves fails closed once an honest NAV outgrows the floor plus that window: `refresh_reference`, open to anyone, steps it forward one window at a time while the NAV is still inside, which the keeper does; beyond, only a two-key re-anchor (S21) recovers. The demo's first fund (haircut 200 bps, floor 10 bps, yield 500 bps, 26 h) needs 22 bps; the widest default envelope (floor 50 bps, yield 1,000 bps, NAV age 3 days) needs 109 bps against a minimum haircut of 100. Tests `a_quiet_month_grants_no_more_growth_than_one_nav_age`, `the_haircut_covers_the_corridor_from_its_bottom_to_its_top`, `a_stale_reference_fails_closed_and_refreshes_step_it_forward`, `the_haircut_must_cover_the_corridor`, `a_new_nav_feed_after_a_quiet_spell_gets_one_nav_age_of_growth` |
| S31 | One precision per currency | **rc5** (threat model Tamper.8). Caps and outstanding totals add base units across the stablecoins of a currency, so these must share one precision: a coin with 6 decimals would multiply every cap by ten. Only stablecoins with 7 decimals (`STABLECOIN_DECIMALS`), those of every Stellar classic asset through its Stellar Asset Contract, are registered: checked at a registration before the first admission, at a proposal and again at its apply, otherwise `StablecoinNotAccepted` (9). Tests `a_stablecoin_needs_seven_decimals_to_be_registered`, `a_stablecoin_needs_seven_decimals_at_its_proposal_and_its_apply` |
| S32 | Feed precision and rounding | **rc5** (threat model Tamper.9). The corridor and the bands are computed in units of the feed: with a coarse feed, bounds rounded outward widened the tolerance beyond the configured basis points. NAV, FX and backup feeds must publish with at least 6 decimals (`MIN_FEED_DECIMALS`), where one unit of a price near 1 is 0.01 bps: checked at admission, at a replacement, when a backup feed is proposed or applied, and when a re-anchor is proposed or applied, otherwise `OutsideEnvelope` (26). The bounds of the corridor and of the bands round inward, and the reference itself always passes. The value of the shares is computed without forming `shares × price`: shares and price are split into whole units and remainders, which gives the direct formula's result wherever that one fits and no overflow for 18-decimal shares priced by an 18-decimal feed. Tests `a_feed_with_fewer_than_six_decimals_is_refused`, `a_reanchor_needs_feeds_of_six_decimals_or_more`, `bounds_round_inward`, `eighteen_decimal_shares_priced_by_an_eighteen_decimal_feed`, and in `test_math.rs` `share_value_is_the_direct_formula_where_it_fits`, `share_value_is_exact_beyond_the_direct_formula`, `within_band_is_the_exact_band` |
| S33 | Valuation recorded per loan | **rc5** (threat model Repud.3). A lender, the issuer or an auditor must be able to evidence what each loan was valued at from the contract's own trail. `open_repo` publishes `RepoValued` right after `RepoOpened`: the NAV read and its time; for a cross price the rate read, the divisor applied (S22) and the rate's time, all 0 for a fund priced directly; the share price in the loan currency; the haircut applied, fund plus FX. When the opening moves the price reference, as `refresh_reference` would, it also publishes `ReferenceUpdated`. A refused opening still leaves no event: a failed transaction publishes none. The indexer's SFTR view takes the haircut from `repo_valued`. Tests `opening_a_repo_publishes_its_valuation_and_any_move_of_the_reference`, `opening_a_cross_priced_repo_publishes_its_fx_leg`, events compared in XDR |
| S34 | Stack size | **rc5** (real-time profile). Every call of the contract allocates its whole linear memory before any work: with the linker's default 1 MiB stack the module starts with 17 pages, which cost 139,698 instructions and 1,114,128 bytes of memory per call. The WASM is now linked with a 64 KiB stack (`contracts/night-window/build.rs`): 2 pages, which saves 122,880 instructions and 983,040 bytes per call. The stack sits below the data in linear memory, so an overflow traps and never overwrites data. Run against the WASM (WASM test mode, `NIGHT_WINDOW_TEST_WASM=1`), the whole contract suite passes with a 2 KiB stack and fails with 1 KiB: a margin above 30. The CI job `wasm-canary` builds the WASM with an 8 KiB stack (`NIGHT_WINDOW_STACK_SIZE=8192`) in a target directory of its own and runs the whole suite against it, so a change that deepens the stack fails there long before it nears the shipped margin. `test_perf_wasm_memory_is_sized` checks the shipped 64 KiB; `test_perf_profile` checks every user-facing call on the release WASM against 12 million instructions, 12 entries written, 8,192 bytes written and 2,048 bytes of events |

### 6.1 Maximum loss per scenario

| Scenario | What limits the loss | Maximum loss |
|---|---|---|
| Manipulated price or compromised oracle | Model-based corridor (S2) whose growth stops at one NAV age, with a haircut that must cover it from its bottom to its top (S30, rc5); FX band bounded by the FX haircut, the rate taken on the lender's side of the band (S22, rc5); feeds of at least 6 decimals, bounds rounded inward (S32, rc5); outside the corridor or the band, refusal. Before rc5 the envelope did not relate the haircut to the corridor, and the cover could fall short once EUR/USD had drifted inside the band or after a long quiet spell of the reference (threat model Tamper.6 and Tamper.7). Residual: a feed that keeps re-stamping a value while the market moves, a NAV, or an FX rate inside the band while the market leaves it, is bounded only by the feeds' integrity, the backup comparison and the monitor (`feed-flat`) | Zero inside the corridor and the band while the market stays inside the band (rc5); cap per fund if the whole oracle is compromised or a frozen feed goes unnoticed |
| Compromised admin key | Native multisig; 48 h delay on every loosening and every upgrade, with a guardian veto that the governor alone cannot lift before 30 days (S7); an upgrade proposed during bootstrap dropped at the first admission (rc5); the pause never blocks an exit (S6); cancellation replayed on testnet 6 s after the proposal | Zero if the guardian watches; otherwise the flows entered after the application |
| Freeze or clawback of the shares by the issuer | Debt settled, shares recorded and delivered when the freeze is lifted (S9, version 3); the exit is never blocked on the share side | Delivery delay; in case of a definitive clawback, the shares clawed back, at most the cap per fund, borne by whichever position closes last (pooled balance, threat model Elev.5) |
| Freeze of a stablecoin by its issuer, on the contract or on a borrower | Nothing in the contract yet: the repurchase in that coin fails while the seizure stays open, and withdrawals in it fail while the contract's balance is frozen (threat model DoS.8, open, an interface change for version 2); MiCA-allowlisted stablecoins only (S5); since rc5 the monitor watches each stablecoin's authorization of the contract, and the incident procedure warns borrowers before their maturity | For a borrower in that coin, the value of its shares above the loan (a forced default); for a lender, its unlent funds in that coin while the freeze lasts |
| Contract bug | Pause of entries; exits intact; lender's reserve (S16) | Value locked at the time of the bug, hence the total cap |
| Lender not authorized to receive the shares | Seized shares go only to an approved holder (S8); since rc5 a revocation no longer blocks the seizure: the debt settles and the shares wait in the contract for an approval or a redirection | Delivery delay; after a revocation followed by `remove_fund`, the shares wait for a re-admission or for a holder approved before the removal (a recovery path is left to version 2) |

### 6.3 DORA: position and requirements retained (13 October 2026)

**Scope.** DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, applies to the financial entities of Article 2, paragraph 1, points (a) to (t): notably management companies, AIF managers, investment firms, electronic money institutions, MiCA providers and ART issuers. The project is not one of them. Nor is it a critical provider designated under Article 31, a designation based on systemic impact, the number of entities served and substitutability.

**What concerns us anyway.** The share and stablecoin issuers the contract works with are financial entities. They remain "at all times fully responsible" (Article 28, paragraph 1) and pass their obligations on by contract. Two cases stand apart, as a delivered firmware stands apart from an operated service:

| Case | Qualification | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| The issuer deploys and operates an instance of the open-source code itself | Software delivery, not a continuous service | Nothing enters its register on account of the project; DORA stays entirely with the issuer |
| The project operates the instance: administration, fund registry, time delay, monitoring, support | ICT service "provided on an ongoing basis" (Article 3, point 21); the project becomes an ICT third-party service provider (Article 3, point 19) | One line in the issuer's register of information (Article 28, paragraph 3); contract compliant with Article 30 |

The qualification of a service as an ICT service depends on the definition of Article 3, point 21: Q&A 2024_7290 (Commission answer published by the EBA on 14 November 2025) applies it to payment service providers and excludes services without a predominant ICT component, but does not say who settles the qualification; the question has been put to counsel (corrected on 15 October 2026). The same goes for the classification as a critical or important function (Article 3, point 22): no official list exists. A capped pilot, whose outage leaves the daily redemption at the fund's counter intact, likely falls under the clauses of Article 30, paragraph 2 only. An instant exit announced to the issuer's clients would likely be classified as important, which adds the clauses of Article 30, paragraph 3.

**Decision D1: design for Article 30, paragraph 3, now.** The extra cost is low at this stage and a late catch-up would be expensive. Each clause is tied to a response:

| Clause | Requirement | Project response | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30(2)(a) | Full description of functions and services, subcontracting | Functional specification; named subcontractors: price provider, RPC, monitoring, host of the indexer | To write |
| 30(2)(b) | Locations of provision and processing | The chain is public and global by nature; monitoring, indexer and hosting declared and located in the European Union | To decide |
| 30(2)(c), (d) | Integrity, confidentiality, access, recovery and return of data | Events per transition (S13), public state, indexer keeping the history; readable export | Partial |
| 30(2)(e) | Service levels | Exits available at all times by design (S6); human response times to quantify | Partial |
| 30(2)(f) | Assistance in case of incident | Named incident manager; notification to the issuer within 4 hours, aligned with its own major-incident reporting deadlines; report within 48 hours | To write |
| 30(2)(g), (h), (i) | Cooperation with authorities, termination, training | Commitment to cooperate; termination without penalty; participation in the issuer's awareness sessions | To write |
| 30(3)(a) | Quantitative performance targets | Settlement time of a transaction; anomaly detection time (S17); availability of exits | To quantify |
| 30(3)(b) | Notice periods and reporting obligations | 48-hour delay on every code or oracle change (S7); public timelock queue | In place by design |
| 30(3)(c) | Tested continuity plans | Pause of entries (S6), end-of-life procedure within 7 days, lender's reserve by agreement (S16); rehearsal on testnet | To test |
| 30(3)(d) | Participation in threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) | Commitment to take part in the issuer's TLPT; STRIDE model and monitoring plan provided | New |

| 28(5) | Information security standards | This security baseline, external audit; independent attestation at scale (SDF standards) | Partial |

**Decision D2: offer both modes.** Instance operated by the project, or instance operated by the issuer from the public code. The second mode is the issuer's exit strategy and reduces its concentration risk (Article 29).


### 6.4 Key custody: policy (17 October 2026)

Every role of the contract (governor, guardian, steward, trustee) is held by an account whose signers can rotate, never by a bare key; the role's address stays stable when the keys change. The governor is a native multisig with thresholds, master key disabled in time, signers on hardware wallets, one of them outside the team; the Soroban host checks its signatures against the account's *medium* threshold. Hot keys (pause, code rent) live in a key management service with an audit log, outside any file. No person holds two roles; the incident manager holds none. Every signer change goes through an atomic transaction, rehearsed on testnet with the same devices, read back on chain and recorded in a written record (`npm run keys` in `demo/`). Rotations follow a written calendar, with a quarterly rotation exercise and an annual restoration exercise from the backups. A compromise playbook per role relies on the delay, the guardian's veto and the fact that exits are never blocked. The full policy follows the lifecycle required by RTS (EU) 2024/1774 (generation, backup, storage, rotation, revocation, destruction) and is kept available to issuers. Status as of 17 October: testnet, governor 2 of 2 with the master key still active; the target above applies to the guarded launch.

### Review rules at every change of the contract

1. For every public function, list every `Address` argument and say which authority every write consumes. Every authority consumed has its `require_auth` in that function.
2. For every external call (token, oracle), say what happens if it fails, if it returns an absurd value, or if it consumes the whole budget.
3. For every computation, show that it uses `checked_*` and in which direction it rounds.
4. For every new storage entry, say who extends its TTL and what happens if it is archived.
5. Every error path has a `try_` test; every privileged function has a test that proves an intruder is refused; every event is compared in XDR.
6. No renumbering of errors, no modification of an existing exported type: we add variants.

- Technical watch (15 October 2026): use soroban-sdk 29.0.1 and never 29.0.0, whose `soroban-env-host` 29.0.0 dependency has been withdrawn; the migration from 28 requires no code change; it comes after the Audit Bank intake, or before it with a new tag, because it changes the hash of the frozen code; Rust 1.98.1 and CLI 28.1.0 staying pinned until the audit. A `try_` call does not catch a called contract that exhausts the CPU budget: a share token or a backup feed that did so would fail the whole transaction instead of being ignored; this is accepted for contracts chosen at admission, and documented. The documentation comments of exported items are compiled into the WASM (about 23 % of its size in version 3): trimming them before freezing the audit footprint also reduces the code rent. The CAP-88 proposal (sub-second ledgers) would shorten TTLs expressed in ledgers: to reassess if it is scheduled.
- Maintenance rule: stay within the two latest major versions of `soroban-sdk`, the only ones that receive security fixes; rebuild and tests at every protocol upgrade; maturities in seconds, never derived from TTLs.

## 8. Framing questions and answers given

1. **Who holds the administration key?** A native 2-of-2 multisig with an outside co-signer on testnet since 15 October 2026 (target: 2-of-3 on hardware devices with one signer outside the team); a sentinel key for the monitoring host since rc4; a single key only in the first days; a native multisig with thresholds and an outside signer from the public testnet on; a time delay in front of upgrades and the oracle before any real deposit, built into the contract since version 3 (S7, S15).
2. **Does the issuer accept that the contract holds shares?** Technically, the contract is an address like any other for an issuer's allowlist: on Stellar, a regulated issuer's token requires the same role on the sender and on the recipient, and that issuer's own redemption contract must hold it. For a classic asset with authorization required, the issuer authorizes the contract's address through `set_authorized`. The business decision remains the issuer's; S8 and S9 set the rules for freeze and seizure.
3. **Does the oracle publish over the weekend?** NAV feeds for fund shares exist on Stellar (RedStone, 18 SEP-40 feeds including BENJI; Pyth); their publication rhythm is a per-fund parameter (S1), complemented by the corridor (S2) and the haircut.
4. **Is a global cap needed?** Yes (S18): per repo, per fund and in total, aligned with the first follow-up audit threshold.
5. **Who decides on the pause?** A single signer, power limited to entries (S6); lenders keep the withdrawal of the part not lent at any time, unless the stablecoin's issuer freezes the contract's balance (DoS.8).

Open questions remaining: the price of a NAV feed for a fund that does not have one yet; the classification of the service as a critical or important function by each issuer (the design targets the most demanding case, decision D1 of section 6.3); the location of the monitoring and the indexer in the European Union; the actual cost of the monitoring.
