# STRIDE threat model of the Open Window contract (crate `night-window`)

Date: 13 October 2026, revised on 20 October 2026 · Status: **kept current with the implementation. The testnet instance runs rc4, on which nineteen drills were rehearsed on 18 October 2026; the rc5 candidate fixes most findings of the security pass of 20 October 2026 (section 3.2) and carries the fixes of its adversarial review of the same day (24 findings), before its tag; its monitor runs nineteen detectors** · Method: Stellar's threat modeling guide (four questions, six STRIDE categories, at least one problem per category, one treatment per problem) · Sibling document: [security.md](security.md), which holds the invariants, the vulnerability classes and decisions S1 to S34.

This document meets the requirements of tranche 2 of the Stellar Community Fund (threat model and monitoring plan) and of the Audit Bank readiness assessment. It is a living tool: it is redone at every architecture change and reread before every audit.

## 1. What are we working on?

### 1.1 Use case

A holder of tokenized money market fund shares (the borrower) locks their shares in the contract and receives stablecoins in the same transaction, for a term and a rate fixed in advance. They repay principal and interest and get their shares back. The stablecoins come from offers deposited in advance by lenders the fund's issuer approves, never the issuer itself (S24): the contract refuses the steward's addresses as lenders, and the attestation each lender co-signs with the steward covers an entity lending through another account (S25). In case of default after maturity, the lender seizes the shares.

### 1.2 External entities

| Entity | Trust | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Borrower | None | Calls `open_repo` and `repurchase` |
| Lender, approved by the fund's issuer and independent of it (an offer may carry the issuer's designation mark, S19) | None | Calls `create_offer`, `withdraw_offer`, `seize` |
| Administrator (multisig, then timelock) | High, constrained | Fund and stablecoin registry, pause, upgrade |
| Fund issuer | External, imposed | Keeps the allowlist of its shares; can freeze or claw back a balance |
| SEP-40 oracle (RedStone, Pyth) | Allowlisted | Publishes the net asset value of the share |
| SEP-41 tokens and SAC (shares, stablecoins) | Allowlisted | Transfer the balances |
| Soroban host, validators | Full | Authorization, reentrancy protection, resource metering, archival |
| Monitoring operator (Hypernative or equivalent) | Medium | Observes the chain; pauses and cancels with the sentinel key, pays rent and steps price references with the keeper's; vetoes only with the guardian's or the trustee's key on its host |
| Indexer, RPC | Low | No contract decision depends on them. The tooling also simulates and submits through the RPC: since rc5 `demo/lib.mjs` signs nothing for the source but its own transaction, signs role authorizations only for the operation's own call, by the signers it expects, with an expiry taken from two endpoints, allows below that call only a `transfer` by the signer into Open Window of the token the call pulls, at its amount, once, refuses a footprint that writes entries the call does not touch (contract code included) and caps the resource fee (25 XLM by default, 100 XLM for a code upload or a restore of the code, 400 XLM for a 30-day slice of the code's rent); with two endpoints, the monitor acts only on what the second confirms, and the verdict on a signed transaction rests on the second endpoint's own answer |

### 1.3 Processes

The contract's entry points, with who may call each, are in [interface-reference.md](interface-reference.md): the lifecycle (`create_offer`, `withdraw_offer`, `open_repo`, `repurchase`, `seize`, `release`), the registries of funds and stablecoins, the price controls, the pause, the governed changes (propose, apply, cancel) and the schema migration. The time delay is built into the contract (S7); there is no separate timelock contract.

### 1.4 Data flows

1. Lender → contract: stablecoins (SEP-41 transfer) and the offer's parameters.
2. Borrower → contract: shares (SEP-41 or SAC transfer); contract → oracle: `lastprice` read; contract → borrower: stablecoins.
3. Borrower → contract: principal and interest; contract → borrower: shares; contract → lender: principal and interest.
4. Lender → contract: `seize`; contract → lender: shares.
5. Admin → contract: registry, pause, upgrade, through the timelock for non-urgent changes.
6. Contract → world: lifecycle events, read by the indexer and the monitoring.

### 1.5 Storage

| Entry | Type | Sensitive content |
|---|---|---|
| `Admin`, `MaxTerm`, `Paused`, `SchemaVersion` | Instance | Authority and global parameters |
| `Fund(address)` | Persistent | Oracle, oracle asset, haircut, freshness, band |
| `Stablecoin(address)` | Persistent | MiCA allowlist |
| `Offer(id)` | Persistent | Lender, stablecoin, available amount, rate, maximum term, accepted funds, sponsor |
| `Repo(id)` | Persistent | Borrower, lender, fund, shares, principal, rate, maturity (timestamp), state |
| `NextOfferId`, `NextRepoId` | Instance | Monotonic counters |

### 1.6 Trust boundaries

- **F1, user ↔ contract**: every argument is hostile; every authority consumed is re-authenticated in the function.
- **F2, contract ↔ tokens**: a token can fail, return a different amount (permissioned token, freeze), or be fake; hence the allowlist and the balance check before and after each transfer.
- **F3, contract ↔ oracle**: the data is external; freshness, model-based corridor, FX band, refusal in case of doubt.
- **F4, admin ↔ contract**: the strongest authority; multisig, timelock, narrow emergency powers.
- **F5, issuer ↔ shares**: the issuer can freeze or claw back; rule S9 sets the consequences.
- **F6, on-chain ↔ off-chain**: monitoring, indexer, web interface; no contract decision depends on them, but the incident response does. A read-only risk assistant (roadmap, Could) would sit on this boundary: its output would be an input to governance, never a signature, and each change it suggests would go through a human proposal and the delay.
- **F7, interface ↔ RPC endpoints and hosting** (added on 20 October 2026): the web interface reads the chain through public RPCs (SDF's, then Gateway.fm's as a fallback) and reads an outside exchange-rate feed directly; it is served from GitHub Pages and signs through the visitor's wallet. Every value decoded from the chain is untrusted input to the page: it is checked at the boundary before it reaches the markup, and the pages carry a content security policy. The RPC providers see the visitor's address and IP; since rc5 the footer of both pages says so and names them.

## 2. What can go wrong?

Each problem is numbered by category. Invariants I1 to I9 and decisions S1 to S34 are those of [security.md](security.md).

### Spoofing

| # | Problem | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Spoof.1 | A third party withdraws an offer, seizes a repo or repurchases on behalf of someone else | `require_auth` on `lender` in `create_offer` and `withdraw_offer`, on `borrower` in `open_repo` and `repurchase`; lender loaded from `Repo` in `seize`. Test: targeted `mock_auths`, an intruder refused, `env.auths()` tree checked |
| Spoof.2 | A fake oracle or a fake token imitates the SEP-40 or SEP-41 interface | Addresses read only from the registry, never from an argument (S5, fund registry) |
| Spoof.3 | Replay of a pre-signed authorization through an intermediary contract | Every layer that exercises an address's authority re-authenticates it; nonces and expirations handled by the host |
| Spoof.4 | A hostile RPC, or an outside price feed the page reads directly, returns a string where the interface expects a number or an id, and the page renders it as markup: a script on the published origin, next to a connected wallet | **Fixed in the web on 20 October 2026** (security pass of 20 October 2026): every decoded value is checked at the boundary (integers, Stellar addresses, repo statuses, SEP-40 prices, decimals between 0 and 38) and the scanned id can no longer be replaced by a decoded field; both pages carry a content security policy (scripts from the site only, connections to the site, the two RPCs and friendbot). Since rc5 the fund, its state, its price and held-back shares are shape-checked too, the deployment file must name the test network (its contract ids are checked for form, not pinned), the History page checks its export's explorer link and decimals, and a translation may not change the HTML tags of its source. Tests: `web/test/boundary.test.js`, `web/test/deployment.test.js`, `web/test/i18n.test.js` |

### Tampering

| # | Problem | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Tamper.1 | Price manipulated to borrow more than the shares are worth (YieldBlox incident, February 2026) | NAV feed, never a DEX price; model-based corridor with a reference that does not follow the feed (S2); freshness (S1); FX band and FX haircut; haircut; refusal outside the corridor. Tests: `reference_cannot_be_ratcheted_by_a_faulty_feed`, `corridor_refuses_a_jump_faster_than_the_maximum_yield` |
| Tamper.2 | Overflow or unfavorable rounding in the computation of the interest or of the borrowing cap | `checked_*` enforced by the lints, `overflow-checks` in release and test, fixed rounding directions (S3, S4), property tests |
| Tamper.3 | Terms of an open repo modified through a registry change | I9: terms frozen at opening; `remove_fund` does not affect open repos |
| Tamper.4 | Malicious or faulty upgrade that corrupts the storage | 48 h timelock (S7), storage keys never renamed or retyped, schema version and `migrate` (S14), upgrade test with the real WASM. Since rc5 an upgrade proposed during bootstrap, whose delay the governor could still shorten, is cancelled at the first admission (test `the_first_admission_drops_a_bootstrap_upgrade`) |
| Tamper.5 | A hostile RPC inflates the resource fee or alters the footprint of a simulated transaction that the wallet then signs | **Partial, narrowed in rc5**: the interface checks the fee of the assembled transaction the wallet signs, not the simulation's summary of it: its resource fee and its total fee beyond the inclusion bid each at most 25 XLM, equal to each other and to the simulation's estimate; the wallet shows the fee before signing. The demo library checks its own writes the same way and bounds their footprint and authorizations (RPC row of 1.2). Open for the web: no allowlist of the footprint and of the restore list; a hostile simulation with consistent fees stays under the cap, so that check is what remains. Tests: `web/test/boundary.test.js`, `demo/test/send.test.mjs` |
| Tamper.6 | The FX band is measured around the rate fixed at admission or at the last re-anchor (S21). Before rc5, once EUR/USD had drifted inside the band, a wrong rate could be accepted while its distance to the market exceeded the FX haircut: S22 bounded the band by the haircut around the reference, not around the market | **Fixed in rc5 (S22)**: the valuation divides the NAV by the higher of the rate and the reference, so a rate below the reference never raises the price, and while the market stays inside the band an admitted rate overvalues the shares by at most the market's move above the reference, at most the band, which the FX haircut covers. Tests `a_wrong_fx_rate_inside_the_band_lends_no_more_than_the_true_value`, `fx_leg_is_priced_at_the_higher_of_the_rate_and_the_reference`. Residual: an FX feed frozen inside the band while the market moves beyond it, bounded only by the feed's integrity, the monitor's `feed-flat` detector (a rate flat over 24 business hours; a second FX source more than half the band away) and a re-anchor (S21) |
| Tamper.7 | The NAV corridor widened with the time since the last reference update; after a quiet spell, a faulty feed or one run by the issuer could move the NAV further than the haircut covers | **Fixed in rc5 (S30)**: the corridor and the model path grow over at most the fund's NAV age since the reference, and the fund's haircut must cover two corridor floors plus one NAV age of growth, at admission, at a replacement and when limits are set (`OutsideEnvelope`); the check of a new feed against the old reference uses the same window. A reference left alone fails closed: the monitor's `reference-age` detector warns and the keeper steps it with `refresh_reference`. The adversarial review of 20 October 2026 (finding VAL-1) found that the same window made every re-anchor under a delay of a week or more fail: the apply now counts the growth since the proposal over the real elapsed time and carries the reference along the model path from the proposed NAV, never below it (S21), and since that one unsigned reading can lift the reference by the growth over the proposal's life, `propose_reanchor` and `apply_reanchor` refuse a fund whose haircut does not cover two floors plus the growth over the delay, the seven-day grace and two NAV ages (S30, `OutsideEnvelope`); the inventory prints that figure per fund under the delay in force. Tests `a_quiet_month_grants_no_more_growth_than_one_nav_age`, `the_haircut_covers_the_corridor_from_its_bottom_to_its_top`, `a_stale_reference_fails_closed_and_refreshes_step_it_forward`, `the_haircut_must_cover_the_corridor`, `a_new_nav_feed_after_a_quiet_spell_gets_one_nav_age_of_growth`, `reanchor_carries_the_readings_of_the_proposal_along_the_model`, `a_reanchor_needs_a_haircut_that_covers_one_wrong_reading_at_its_apply`. Residual: a NAV re-stamped flat while the true value falls, bounded only by the feeds' integrity, the backup comparison and `feed-flat` (a NAV flat over two business days); each wrong reading a refresh accepts lifts the reference by at most one NAV age of growth (S2); a re-anchor pending across the upgrade to rc5 on a fund below the new cover fails `OutsideEnvelope` at its apply (none on testnet) |
| Tamper.8 | Caps and outstanding totals add raw base units across the stablecoins of one currency: a stablecoin with other decimals would distort them | **Fixed in rc5 (S31)**: only 7-decimal stablecoins are registered, at a registration, at a proposal and at its apply (`StablecoinNotAccepted`); both testnet stablecoins have 7. Normalising units across precisions is left to a later version. Tests `a_stablecoin_needs_seven_decimals_to_be_registered`, `a_stablecoin_needs_seven_decimals_at_its_proposal_and_its_apply` |
| Tamper.9 | Extreme decimals (18-decimal shares with an 18-decimal feed) could overflow `max_principal`; coarse feeds widened the tolerances, since the bounds rounded outward | **Fixed in rc5 (S32)**: NAV, FX and backup feeds need 6 to 18 decimals at admission, at a replacement, for a backup feed and for a re-anchor (`OutsideEnvelope`; the upper bound from the adversarial review's finding VAL-3, since a feed with more could be admitted and then never price); the bounds round inward; the value of the shares is computed without overflow by splitting shares and price, and the cross price is the exact floor of `nav × 10^d / rate` whatever the rate, through a 256-bit product where the direct one overflows (`math::mul_div_floor_wide`). Tests `a_feed_with_fewer_than_six_decimals_is_refused`, `a_feed_with_more_than_eighteen_decimals_is_refused`, `a_reanchor_needs_feeds_of_six_decimals_or_more`, `bounds_round_inward`, `eighteen_decimal_shares_priced_by_an_eighteen_decimal_feed`, `an_eighteen_decimal_cross_fund_prices_a_thousand_dollar_share`, `an_eighteen_decimal_rate_above_one_hundred_seventy_prices_every_nav`, and the property tests of `test_math.rs` |
| Tamper.10 | A backup NAV feed could be the primary feed itself, and one proposed or applied while the primary was stale was never compared with it: the redundancy was nominal (finding oracle-5 of the security pass) | **Fixed in rc5 (S1)**: a backup naming the primary's oracle and asset is refused (`OutsideEnvelope`); its proposal and its apply need a fresh primary it agrees with (`StalePrice`, `NoPrice` or `PriceDivergence` otherwise). A replacement that promotes the backup's feed to primary removes the backup when it applies (`FallbackRemoved` by the contract itself; adversarial review finding VAL-4), which the monitor reports as a promotion, not an attack. A fund without a backup whose primary feed died leaves through `remove_fund`, `set_fund` on the new feed, a re-anchor, then both applies after one delay (VAL-2). Tests `the_primary_feed_cannot_be_its_own_fallback`, `a_fallback_is_installed_only_against_a_fresh_primary`, `promoting_the_fallback_to_primary_removes_it`, `a_dead_nav_feed_without_fallback_is_replaced_through_a_removal`. Open: the tolerance is capped by the envelope's corridor floor, not by the fund's own |

### Repudiation

| # | Problem | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Repud.1 | A lender or an issuer disputes a seizure or an amount | One event per transition with identifiers and addresses as topics (S13), compared in XDR in the tests; an indexer keeping the history beyond the RPC's 7 days |
| Repud.2 | The administrator changes a parameter without a trace | An event on every administrative change, public timelock queue |
| Repud.3 | Before rc5, the valuation behind each loan (price, exchange rate, applied haircut) was in no event, and a refused opening leaves no trace on chain | **Fixed in rc5 (S33)**: `open_repo` publishes `RepoValued` (NAV and its time, whether the backup feed supplied it, FX rate read and divisor applied, price, haircut) after `RepoOpened`, and `ReferenceUpdated` when it moves the reference; the indexer's SFTR view takes the applied haircut from it and notes an opening priced by the backup feed, which the monitor reports as a warning. A refused opening still leaves no event, since a failed transaction publishes none. Tests `opening_a_repo_publishes_its_valuation_and_any_move_of_the_reference`, `opening_a_cross_priced_repo_publishes_its_fx_leg`, `an_opening_records_whether_the_fallback_priced_it` |

| # | Problem | Treatment |
|---|---|---|

### Denial of Service

| # | Problem | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| DoS.1 | An operation exceeds the resource limits because it walks through every position | I6: one entry per offer and per repo, no global loop; at most 8 funds per offer (S10); load test at constant cost |
| DoS.2 | Archived entry (expired TTL) that blocks a repurchase or a seizure | Deadlines as timestamps, never as TTLs; TTL extended on every write; extension task in operations; restoration possible by the caller |
| DoS.3 | Oracle silent or stale over a weekend | Explicit refusal (`NoPrice`, `StalePrice`) rather than a default price; freshness parameter per fund; second oracle in the next version |
| DoS.4 | Abusive pause that blocks exits | The pause only affects `create_offer` and `open_repo` (S6); `repurchase`, `withdraw_offer` and `seize` always remain available |
| DoS.5 | Freeze or clawback of the shares by the issuer during a repo | Rule S9: debt maintained. Today, the repurchase fails as long as the issuer freezes the contract's shares; limit documented and covered by a test; closing without restitution planned for version 1.1 |
| DoS.6 | Repos of a few seconds that probe or saturate the contract | Minimum term of one hour (S11), caps (S18) |
| DoS.7 | The steward revokes the lender after an opening: before rc5, `seize` required a current approval of its destination, so the matured repo could no longer be seized and the borrower kept the choice to repay or walk away; after `remove_fund` the block was permanent | **Fixed in rc5 (S8)**: the lender of record seizes without a current approval; the default settles, and while the lender is not approved the shares wait as a `PendingRelease` (`release` returns `false`) until the steward approves it again or the lender redirects them to an approved holder. The monitor warns when a lender with open repos is revoked (`lenders`) and reports each waiting delivery once (`reconciliation`). Tests `a_revoked_lender_still_seizes_and_its_shares_wait_for_an_approval`, `a_revoked_lender_redirects_its_seized_shares_to_an_approved_holder`, `a_revoked_lender_seizes_on_a_removed_fund`, `seized_shares_never_go_to_the_steward`. Residual: after a revocation followed by `remove_fund`, the shares wait until a re-admission, or until the lender redirects them to a holder approved before the removal; a recovery path for a removed fund is left to version 2 |
| DoS.8 | The stablecoin issuer freezes the contract or a borrower: the repurchase fails while the seizure stays open, which forces a default; the cash side has no equivalent of S9 | **Open, contract with an interface change (V2)**, confirmed: a grace period or a deferred settlement when the cash leg is refused. Meanwhile: MiCA-allowlisted stablecoins only; since rc5 the monitor reads each registered stablecoin's authorization of the contract and raises a critical alert when it is revoked (`stablecoins`), and the incident procedure has a stablecoin-freeze row (contact the issuer, warn the borrowers before their maturity) |
| DoS.9 | On mainnet, the minimum lifetime of a restored entry is about 120 days: a contract code entry left to archive may cost more to restore than one transaction can carry, which would block every exit | **Partly treated in rc5's tooling**: the monitor alerts when the code entry has less than 90 days to live (14 days for the other entries), the keeper extends it 30 days at a time, up to 120 days, each extension under a 400 XLM fee cap (a dearer slice is shortened), and the keeper's float alert is 800 XLM, one upgrade run (which keeps the new code alive to 91 days, about 84 days of rent, 706 XLM at the 8.40 XLM a day read on testnet in the afternoon of 20 October 2026) with the rent up to 9.5 XLM a day; an archived code is restored only by `npm run upgrade -- --restore-code`, for the code two endpoints agree the instance runs. **To check before any mainnet deployment**: a simulated restore of the code entry, read only; S20 could still be reconsidered (a capped extension on user paths). Not reachable on testnet, where the minimum is 7 days |
| DoS.10 | The monitor's `--act` mode turned conditions it cannot attribute (a donated share unit, a reverting feed, one lying RPC) into instance-wide pauses; its single `getLedgerEntries` batch failed as the state grew | **Mostly treated in rc5's tooling**: a surplus is a warning and only a deficit pauses; one fund's failing read is that fund's critical alert while the others keep running; ledger entries are read 200 keys at a time; every automatic action waits for a second endpoint's own reading and becomes a critical alert for a human when it disagrees; a veto needs its key on the host. Tests in `demo/test/`. Open: `scan()` still reads repos and offers one simulation per id (finding econ-3, second half); no spending budget per window for `--act` |
| DoS.11 | A lender lends to itself and holds a fund's and a currency's cap at almost no cost | **Open, economic**: a single-lender instance (mode D3) is not exposed; elsewhere, caps per lender or a fee; to decide |

### Elevation of Privilege

| # | Problem | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Elev.1 | Capture of the admin through a replayable initialization function | Ruled out: `__constructor` constructor, no `initialize` |
| Elev.2 | Compromise of the admin key | Native threshold multisig with an outside signer; 48 h timelock on upgrade and oracle; two-step admin transfer (S7); emergency powers limited to entries. Since rc5 the trustee and the sentinel stay independent of every other role, checked both ways, live pending roles included, and the sentinel signs its own naming so that a guardian cannot bar the trustee's successor or the governor's forced candidate by naming it (S26, S28; tests `trustee_cannot_be_a_proposed_guardian_or_the_sentinel`, `sentinel_cannot_be_given_another_role`, `naming_a_sentinel_takes_its_own_signature`, `an_expired_appointment_no_longer_blocks_the_trustee_or_the_sentinel`); `accept_admin` and `apply_forced_guardian_change` do not check again, which matters only for a proposal made under rc4 |
| Elev.3 | A sponsor lender obtains an exit or seizure privilege | S19: the sponsor mark is indicative, set by the steward, with no on-chain matching; same rules as any offer |
| Elev.4 | A lender not authorized by the issuer receives seized shares and blocks them | S8: only lenders approved by the steward may lend; checked at `create_offer`, again at every `open_repo`, and on the destination of `seize`, except the lender of record, whose seized shares wait in the contract until it is approved again or redirects them (rc5); `release` delivers seized shares only to an approved holder, never the steward |
| Elev.5 | The shares of a fund are pooled in the contract: a clawback by the issuer aimed at one position is borne by whichever position closes last | **Open, contract with an interface change (V2)**, confirmed: per-position accounting of clawbacks. Meanwhile, the monitor's reconciliation detector raises a critical alert and pauses entries on a deficit of the pooled balance against the open repos and pending releases |
| Elev.6 | A stablecoin registration proposed by a governor survived that governor's handover: these proposals are kept per token and cannot be listed, so the handover could not drop them, and anyone could apply one once due (finding auth-3 of the security pass) | **Fixed in rc5 (S29)**: only the governor in office applies a registration (`apply_stablecoin_change`), so one proposed by a replaced governor applies only if its successor takes it up; the guardian, the sentinel or the new governor can cancel it. Test `a_stablecoin_proposed_by_a_replaced_governor_needs_its_successor` |

## 3. What are we going to do about it?

The treatments above fall into three states:

State as of 14 October 2026, lifecycle implemented:

- **In place and tested**: Spoof.1, Spoof.2, Spoof.3, Tamper.1, Tamper.2, Tamper.3, Repud.2, Disc.2, DoS.1, DoS.2, DoS.3, DoS.4, DoS.6, Elev.1, Elev.3, Elev.4. The caps per repo, per fund and per currency are in place.
- **Partial**: Repud.1 (events in place, indexer to build), Tamper.4 (upgrade reserved to the governor; time delay and test with a real WASM to do), DoS.5 (limit documented, treatment in version 1.1).
- **To put in place before any real deposit**: Elev.2 (multisig, time delay), the monitoring plan below in production, the lender's reserve (S16).

The monitoring plan is implemented (`demo/monitor.mjs`) and was rehearsed on testnet on 14 October 2026: six detectors out of seven were fired by a real event, the planned action was taken and the state restored. Results and what to do in [incident-procedure.md](incident-procedure.md).

The schedule of these steps is in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md).

### 3.1 Monitoring plan, first version

The plan follows from the model: every threat that cannot be made impossible by the code gets a detector and an action.

| Threat | Detector | Threshold | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tamper.1 | The contract's own valuation of each fund (`quote`), and the NAV's age | A price refusal, or a failure inside the valuation; a NAV two hours from its age bound | Critical: pause of entries by the sentinel, once a second endpoint reads the same refusal; a warning for the age |
| Elev.2 | Operations scheduled in the timelock; calls to `set_admin`, `upgrade`, `set_fund` | Any occurrence | Immediate alert; verification by a second signer during the delay; an unannounced upgrade or stablecoin proposal is cancelled |
| DoS.2 | Remaining TTL of the open `Fund`, `Offer`, `Repo` entries, and of the `Lender` approvals an exit reads (seizure, redirection): an archived approval would block a seizure, which S6 forbids; the contract code (DoS.9) | Less than 14 days; the code less than 90 days (rc5) | Automatic extension, the code in 30-day slices up to 120 days; alert if the failure repeats |
| Tamper.1 (frozen NAV) | NAV published with a fresh timestamp but an unchanged value, publication after publication (the age check does not see it); threshold per fund, a constant-NAV fund publishes the same value by design | Unchanged for more than 72 hours | Warning; check the fund type and that the operator is publishing; the corridor floor bounds the exposure |
| DoS.1, DoS.6 | Openings in the last hour; outstanding against each fund's and each currency's cap | More than 20 openings within the hour; 80 % of a cap | Warning; pause of entries if unexplained |
| DoS.5 | Share balance of the contract per fund against the open repos and the pending releases (I2), and since rc5 each stablecoin's balance against the offers (I1) | A deficit; a surplus | Deficit: critical, pause of entries (a clawback, a freeze or a fault); surplus: a warning with its size (a transfer that no function moves out). A waiting delivery is reported once per reason, and the keeper releases one that has become deliverable |
| Repud.1 | Expected event missing after a successful transaction | Any occurrence | Alert; investigation |
| Tamper.1 (FX) | Exchange rate published with a fresh timestamp but an unchanged value (closed market: the age check does not see it) | Unchanged for more than 60 hours | Warning; check that the market is closed; the FX band and haircut bound the exposure |
| DoS.2 (rent) | Balance of the keeper account that pays the extensions | Below 800 XLM: the rent of one upgrade run (about 84 days of the code, 706 XLM on testnet at 8.40 XLM a day, 20 October 2026) with the rent up to 9.5 XLM a day, which also covers the routine two slices | Warning; top up from the treasury |
| Infra.1 (reading) | Ledger height compared across the configured RPCs (`STELLAR_RPC_URLS`); since rc5, the pause flag and the pending proposals read on a second endpoint | A silent RPC, or more than 10 ledgers apart; any disagreement on those facts | Warning for the heights, and the monitoring fails over; critical for a disagreement: nothing automatic rests on a fact the endpoints disagree on, and every action waits for the second endpoint's own reading |
| Repud.2 (journal) | Since rc5, the indexer's journal compared both ways and by content with the events of an endpoint other than the indexer's, over the ledgers both cover | An event served but not journaled; a journal line that endpoint does not know, or whose ledger, transaction, topics or data differ | Warning for a missing line: rerun the indexer. Critical for an unknown or altered line: stop trusting the journal from that line and re-index from an honest endpoint |
| Elev.2 (schema, rc4) | `schema_version` compared with the version the code expects | Stored version lower | Critical alert: the upgrade was not followed by `migrate`; entries refused (`SchemaOutdated`), exits open |
| Tamper.7 (reference, rc5) | `reference-age`: age of each fund's price reference against its NAV age bound | Older than the bound | Warning; with `--act` the keeper calls `refresh_reference` when the NAV is newer; a NAV already outside the corridor needs a two-key re-anchor |
| Tamper.6, Tamper.7 (flat feeds, rc5) | `feed-flat`: a NAV or an FX rate re-stamped with the same value, counted on business days; the FX leg against a second FX source | A NAV flat over 2 business days; an FX rate flat over 24 business hours; the two FX sources more than half the band apart | Warning; verify with the operator; re-anchor or move the FX leg if the fund's feed is wrong |
| DoS.8 (rc5) | `stablecoins`: whether each registered stablecoin still authorizes the contract's balance | Deauthorized | Critical alert, no pause (entries in that coin fail anyway); contact the issuer; warn the borrowers of open repos in that coin before their maturity |
| DoS.7 (rc5) | `lenders`: a lender revoked while its repos are open | Any occurrence, and again when the number of open repos changes | Warning; ask the steward; after maturity the lender seizes, then is approved again or redirects the release |
| Elev.2 (proposals, rc5) | `proposals`: the pending upgrade, governor handover and forced guardian change, read from state every pass | Unannounced | Critical on every pass until gone; cancellation by the sentinel, veto by the guardian or the trustee when its key is on the host |
| DoS.10 (funds, rc5) | `funds`: a fund the contract no longer lists, or whose reads fail | Any occurrence | Critical for that fund, no pause; the other funds and detectors keep running |

**Implementation as of 18 October 2026**: `demo/monitor.mjs` carries twelve detectors, each run per admitted fund, and seventeen drills rehearsed on testnet (15 of 15 on 17 October, 16 and 17 added on the 18th), a witness mode (`--witness`) for a second instance that alerts without ever acting (`npm run monitor -- --rehearse`), with automatic reaction on critical alerts (`--act`), a failover across several RPCs (`STELLAR_RPC_URLS`) and an optional heartbeat (`HEARTBEAT_URL`). The event history beyond the RPC's retention is kept by `indexer/` (append-only journal under a hash chain, verifiable). Tools considered for operation on dedicated infrastructure: Hypernative (SDF guide of July 2026, two published examples) or OctoPos; alerts to the incident lead; preliminary report within 48 hours in case of incident, public post-mortem.

**rc5 tooling (20 October 2026)**: the detectors moved to `demo/detectors.mjs`, pure functions tested offline (`npm test` in `demo/`, CI job `tooling`). There are nineteen: `funds`, `price`, `nav`, `reference-age`, `reconciliation`, `stablecoins`, `lenders`, `privileged`, `proposals`, `pause`, `ttl`, `exposure`, `fx`, `feed-flat`, `keeper`, `events`, `rpc`, `schema`, `journal`; those about a fund run for every admitted fund. Reads simulate from the null account, so the monitoring host holds no governor key. With two endpoints in `STELLAR_RPC_URLS` (the default lists two public testnet RPCs), every automatic action waits for the second endpoint's own reading; a veto needs the guardian's or the trustee's key on the host, and a planned handover or forced guardian change is announced first (`--announce-admin=`, `--announce-guardian=`) so that it is never vetoed. The nineteen drills were rehearsed against rc4; the detectors added since have offline tests and no drill yet, and the rehearsal against rc5 follows its governed upgrade. After the adversarial review of 20 October 2026: the monitor merges its state file (`monitor.json`) before every pass and decision, so an announcement made while it runs needs no restart, and writes it under a lock (`.lock`, stale after 10 s, a writer that cannot take it in 15 s stops) and a rename, so a watch and an upgrade script never lose each other's writes; stablecoin proposals are read from state every pass and cancelled only while pending; a `FallbackRemoved` published by the contract itself is reported as a promotion, an opening priced by the backup feed (`RepoValued.fallback`) as a warning, and an archived code entry points at `--restore-code`; a disposable instance (`OPEN_WINDOW_STATE=<name>`, letters, digits, `_` and `-` only) keeps its own monitor file.

### 3.2 Security pass of 20 October 2026

A zero-day hunt over the contract (rc4, unchanged since the tag), its RPC path, the web interface and the tooling, following the initial approach of [security.md](security.md) and a review of the 2025-2026 attack landscape (price-source manipulation such as YieldBlox, configuration and unit errors such as Moonwell, risk-parameter pushes such as Aave's CAPO incident, front-end and supply-chain attacks). Eight lenses (arithmetic and units; authorization, governance and upgrade; oracles; token interactions; state, storage and lifetime; economic attacks; web and hosting; RPC trust and tooling), each finding then put to an adversarial verifier.

- **Findings**: 50 reported. The 11 of the arithmetic and token lenses were confirmed by their verifier; the others were triaged by hand, and the most serious were checked against the code.
- **Fixed the same day** (web and tooling, no contract change): Spoof.4, Tamper.5 (partly), Disc.4.
- **Fixed in the rc5 candidate** (the same day, not yet tagged): in the contract, DoS.7, Tamper.6, Tamper.7, Tamper.8, Tamper.9, Tamper.10, Repud.3 and Elev.6, with decisions S30 to S34 and the amended S1, S7, S8, S22, S26, S28 and S29 of [security.md](security.md), plus the bootstrap upgrade (Tamper.4) and the two-way independence of the trustee and the sentinel (Elev.2); in the tooling, DoS.10 (mostly), Infra.1 and Repud.2 (content checked on a second endpoint), Infra.2, Infra.4, Tamper.5 (narrowed), Infra.3 (partly) and Disc.3 (disclosed on the page). The rows above describe each class and its fix, not a way to exploit it: the instance runs on testnet and holds nothing of value.

- **Still open**: DoS.8 and Elev.5 (contract, an interface change for version 2), DoS.11 (economic), the open parts of DoS.10, Tamper.5 and Infra.3, and the residual risks below.

| # | Problem | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Infra.2 | The web failed over only on a timeout or a network failure, not on staleness: a fast but lagging endpoint would serve old state | **Fixed in rc5 (web)**: before each refresh the page polls every endpoint's latest ledger (1.5 s per pass) and judges lag by the endpoint's own close time against the local clock: calls leave one more than 10 ledgers behind and more than 60 s old; a read that fails on the network leaves at once, one that times out is retried once and leaves on the second timeout within 30 s; the endpoint left rests 60 s, doubling up to 10 min when it fails again soon after; the first endpoint takes the calls back once level and rested (two height polls answered in a row); while every other endpoint rests the page stays where it is, one move at most per rest. An HTTP 429 is waited out on the same endpoint (Retry-After capped at 5 s, else about 1 s), and the third in a row, a Retry-After above 5 s or three 429 height polls in a row leave it; the page sends at most 20 calls a second to one endpoint (Gateway.fm allows 30 per second per IP, shared behind a venue's NAT) and keeps at most 6 in flight. The console and the toasts say every move with its reason. Test `web/test/rpc.test.js` |
| Infra.3 | Front-end supply chain and hosting: npm install scripts, fresh releases, a github.io origin shared with the account's other Pages sites | **Partial, narrowed in rc5**: lockfile and `npm ci --ignore-scripts` in CI for the web, the demo and the indexer; a gate on high or critical advisories of the web's runtime dependencies (`npm audit --omit=dev --audit-level=high`) before the build and the publication; `persist-credentials: false` on every checkout; actions pinned to a SHA; Dependabot; content security policy; and a refusal to run inside another site's frame (`web/src/frame.js`). The frame check is defence in depth only: a sibling Pages site on the same origin can still script the page or call the wallet, so a dedicated origin stays the fix. Open: that origin, and a release-age gate on npm updates |
| Infra.4 | A hostile endpoint reports success for a write it never forwarded, and the page says it is confirmed: a repurchase near maturity could be lost that way | **Fixed in rc5 (web)**: a write shows as confirmed only once a second endpoint sees it; until then the page says "Submitted, confirming". A second endpoint's silence is told from its contradiction (adversarial review, finding WEB-1): when no other endpoint answered within about 30 s the write is reported in amber as applied according to the endpoint in use, with no second confirmation, and the page is free at once; `TxUnconfirmed` is kept for a contradiction (a failure reported there, or a transaction unknown past its validity window, confirmed by asking the endpoint in use again) or a live endpoint that never sees it by the end of the window plus 30 s; `TxFailed` when both report a failure. The fee is checked on the assembled transaction. Residual: a write only one endpoint saw rests on that endpoint's word, and the page says so. Test `web/test/confirm.test.js` |

Residual risks after rc5, each recorded in its row above:

- an FX feed frozen inside the band while the market moves beyond it (Tamper.6), and a NAV re-stamped flat while the true value falls (Tamper.7): bounded only by the feeds' integrity, the backup comparison and the monitor (`feed-flat`);
- seized shares waiting after a revocation followed by `remove_fund` (DoS.7), until a recovery path for a removed fund in version 2;
- each wrong reading a refresh accepts lifts the reference by at most one NAV age of growth (Tamper.7, S2), outside the re-anchor cover, which is sized for the apply alone;
- a re-anchor pending across the upgrade to rc5 on a fund whose haircut is below the new cover fails `OutsideEnvelope` at its apply (Tamper.7; none on testnet);
- a backup feed's tolerance capped by the envelope's corridor floor rather than the fund's own (Tamper.10);
- `accept_admin` and `apply_forced_guardian_change` do not check the independence of the trustee and the sentinel again (Elev.2), which matters only for a proposal made under rc4;
- no allowlist of the footprint or of the restore list for the web's writes (Tamper.5);
- `scan()` still reads repos and offers one simulation per id (DoS.10);
- a web write that only one endpoint saw rests on that endpoint's word, said in amber (Infra.4); with a single endpoint configured, the tooling's verdicts on a signed transaction rest on its word too (Infra.1); and the frame refusal is defence in depth only (Infra.3).
